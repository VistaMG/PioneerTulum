01 The opportunity

Land is the part of Tulum they stopped making.

Tulum's growth story is well covered. The part that matters to a developer is narrower: how much contiguous, clean-title land is still available inside the corridor that guests actually want to be in — and what it now costs to build there responsibly.

Supply

Contiguous scale, single title

Parcels of this size inside the beach-road corridor are rarely offered whole. Assembling 1.4 hectares lot-by-lot means multiple sellers, multiple title histories and multiple closings. Here it is one transaction.

Access

The corridor got a lot closer

Felipe Carrillo Puerto International Airport (TQO) opened on Tulum's doorstep in December 2023, the Tren Maya links the peninsula's destinations, and Highway 307 continues to be upgraded. Arrival friction — the historic brake on Tulum's rate ceiling — has fallen.

Demand

Sustainability now prices in

Water, energy and canopy have moved from marketing copy to underwriting. Projects that can evidence how they treat wastewater and protect the aquifer are increasingly the ones that hold rate, secure operators and survive scrutiny at exit.

Structure

Enter at land basis, phase the rest

Buying at land basis lets you stage capital: hold, sell serviced lots, or build vertically in phases as absorption allows. The subdivision is already drawn, so the exit options exist on day one rather than after a re-plat.

04 The build code

Luxury the aquifer can live with.

Tulum's real constraint isn't design, it's the ground underneath it — a limestone shelf threaded with cenotes and underground rivers that carry whatever a project puts into them. Pioneer Tulum sells land with a design and sustainability code attached, recorded against every lot. It binds you, and it binds your neighbour.

Canopy

Vegetation retained, not replaced

Mature trees are surveyed and tagged before any clearing, and a defined share of each lot stays as existing growth. The road follows the terrain rather than flattening it.

Water out

Treatment before the karst

On-lot wastewater treatment to a specified standard, with no untreated discharge to the limestone. This is the single decision that most affects the cenotes downstream.

Water in

Capture, store, reuse

Roof capture and cistern storage sized to the build, plus greywater reuse for irrigation — cutting reliance on trucked and municipal supply through the dry season.

Energy

Solar-ready by specification

Orientation, roof structure, conduit and inverter space specified up front, so photovoltaic and battery become a fit-out decision instead of an expensive retrofit.

Material

A regional palette

Chukum and local limestone, lime plasters, regional hardwood and thatch where it belongs. Materials that age well in salt air and read as Yucatán rather than imported.

Night

Dark-sky lighting

Shielded, warm and low — enough to move safely, not enough to erase the sky or disrupt the animals that make the jungle worth living beside.

A project should be invisible to the cenote downstream.

05 Buying land here

What buying land in Tulum actually involves.

This parcel is titled private property, held in a Mexican company and registered with the municipality. The sequence below is the one we run, and everything in step four is evidenced before you are asked to commit.

01

NDA and package

A short confidentiality agreement, then the investment package: survey, title history, land-use certificate, service letters, lot schedule, the build code and pricing.

02

Walk the parcel

Boundaries, canopy, road access, the cenote path and the surrounding corridor — on foot, with the plan in hand. Remote buyers get a filmed walkthrough.

03

Offer and reservation

A letter of intent fixes price, scope, timeline and exclusivity. A reservation deposit holds the lots off market while diligence runs.

04

Due diligence

Your notary and attorney verify the chain of title, the certificate of freedom from liens, property tax and water clearances, the origin of the title, the survey and the land-use certificate.

05

Choose the vehicle

The parcel is already held in a Mexican company, so a buyer can take the land as an asset purchase or acquire the company itself. The two routes differ on transfer tax, liability and timing — your counsel picks against your tax position and exit plan.

06

Close before the notary

The deed is signed before a notario público, funds move through escrow, transfer tax is paid, and the deed is recorded at the public property registry.

07

Permits and construction

Land-use, environmental and municipal construction licences, then service connections and build — with our vetted architects, contractors and project managers if you want them.

08

Operate or exit

Hold, place with an operator, or sell. Introductions to rental managers, hotel operators and brokers active in the corridor come with the relationship.
